\section*{Structure of the Paper}
The structure of the paper will be organized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Preliminaries:} This section introduces the necessary notation,
concepts related to PI and PIETOOLS, and the IQC result that will be central to
the subsequent developments in the paper.

\item \textbf{Scaled IO Result:} In this section, we define the scalings
associated with both the primal and dual systems. These scalings play a critical
role in the connection between the primal and dual formulations. Then we will
use these scalings in a scaled IO duality result.

\item \textbf{Connection to IQC:} We then connect the scaled duality result to
IQC. Consider the following hard IQC condition:
\[
\int_0^T \left\langle \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix},
\Pi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix} \right\rangle \, dt
\geq 0
\]
where the dynamic multiplier \( \Pi(j\omega) \) can be decomposed into a dynamic
part \( \Psi(j\omega) \) and a static part \( \mathcal{V} \). The IQC condition
then becomes:
\[
\int_0^T \left\langle \Psi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t)
\end{bmatrix}, \mathcal{V} \Psi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t)
\end{bmatrix} \right\rangle \, dt \geq 0
\]
We propose a further decomposition of \( \mathcal{V} \) as:
\[
\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{S}^* \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ 0 & -I \end{bmatrix}
\mathcal{S}
\]
where \( \mathcal{S} \) represents the scaling operations applied to the output
of \( \Psi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix} \). Assuming
\( \Psi \) admits a PIE system, we augment the nominal system as \( z_\Delta = G
w_\Delta \). Rewriting the IQC condition with this decomposition gives:
\[
\int_0^T \left\langle \mathcal{S} \Psi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\
w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ 0 & -I \end{bmatrix}
\mathcal{S} \Psi \begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix}
\right\rangle \, dt
\]
This formulation reduces to analyzing the open-loop system \( \mathcal{S} \Psi
\begin{bmatrix} z_\Delta(t) \\ w_\Delta(t) \end{bmatrix} \). After establishing
the necessary assumptions and conditions, we apply the scaled duality result to
the augmented system with corresponding scalings.

\item \textbf{Controller Synthesis:} In this section, we propose Linear PI
Inequalities (LPIs) for robustly synthesizing controller gains. If time allows,
we will extend this to include output feedback. Additionally, we may explore
using the DK iteration method to solve for suboptimal solutions when combining
dynamic multipliers with output feedback.

\item \textbf{Examples and Validation:} Finally, we explore practical
applications, focusing on performance shaping using weighting filters. The
results will be validated through Frequency Response Function (FRF)
measurements.
\end{itemize}
