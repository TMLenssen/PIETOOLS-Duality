The broader system-theoretic foundations include the semigroup treatments of
Curtain and Pritchard~\cite{curtain1978infinite} and Curtain and
Zwart~\cite{curtain1995introduction}, and the well-posed linear systems
framework of Staffans~\cite{staffans2005wellposed}.
Earlier infinite-dimensional robust control includes stabilization by
finite-dimensional controllers~\cite{curtain1986robust} and state-space
$H_\infty$ synthesis for distributed-parameter systems~\cite{vankeulen1993hinfinity}.

Related PDE methods include backstepping~\cite{krstic2008boundary},
robust Lyapunov-based control~\cite{fridman2012sampled}, and semidefinite
stability analysis~\cite{valmorbida2016pdesdp}. PIE representations provide
an operator framework for PDE analysis~\cite{peet2021pierepresentation,shivakumar2024GDPE},
with duality enabling nominal state-feedback synthesis~\cite{shivaknew}.

IQCs describe uncertainty~\cite{megretski1997system} and connect to storage
inequalities through dissipativity~\cite{willems1972dissipative1,6915700}.
PIE-based developments cover robust ODE--PDE analysis~\cite{9303892},
infinite-dimensional hard IQCs~\cite{10156335}, and structured robustness,
performance, and observer synthesis~\cite{lenssen2025muanalysissynthesisframeworkpartial}.

Dual IQCs support robust feedforward design~\cite{kose2009robust} and LPV
estimator and feedforward synthesis~\cite{venkataraman}. Convex synthesis
depends on the system structure~\cite{scherer2009robust}; general dynamic
multiplier methods use alternating design and analysis~\cite{veenman2014iqcsynthesis,wang2016robustiqc}.
Dynamic-IQC feedback also addresses uncertain systems with state delay~\cite{7353149}.

For finite-horizon certificates, relevant connections include doubly-hard
factorizations~\cite{carrasco2018equivalence}, IQCs with terminal
costs~\cite{scherer2018dynamicdissipation}, and multiplier storage that reduces
conservatism~\cite{magicalhiddenenergy,hu}.
