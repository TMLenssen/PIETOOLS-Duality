The controller synthesis problem is to find $K$ and an admissible IQC
certificate that establish robust stability and performance of the
closed-loop interconnection. The primal component block in
\eqref{eq:primal-system-components} shows why the primal analysis condition
does not directly yield a convex synthesis condition. The controller gains
$\mcl K_{\mathrm p}$ and $\mcl K_\Theta$ enter both the state dynamics
and the filtered outputs. When these gains are decision operators, the
storage inequality contains products of the storage operator $\mcl P$
and the controller gains.

For ODE systems, a congruence transformation with $\mcl P^{-1}$,
combined with a change of variables for the controller gain multiplied by
$\mcl P^{-1}$, removes this bilinearity from the
dynamics~\cite{zhou1996robust}. For the primal
PIE realization, however, the storage operator is preceded by the adjoint
of the reconstruction operator. Consequently, the same congruence leaves
the factor
\[
    \mcl P^{-1}
    \bmat{\mcl T_{\mathrm P}^*&0\\0&\mcl T^*}
    \mcl P.
\]
For ODEs the middle factor is the identity, and the storage operators
cancel. For PIEs these operators do not generally commute, so this
cancellation is unavailable and the transformed inequality remains
nonconvex. Thus, although the congruence is well-defined for coercive
$\mcl P$, it cannot in general be used to obtain a convex synthesis
condition as in the ODE case. Applying an inverse of the reconstruction
operator does not generally resolve this issue either, since this inverse
need not be bounded on the underlying $L_2$ space. These structural issues
motivate the use of PIE duality for state-feedback
synthesis~\cite{shivaknew}.

A second obstacle arises when the controller $K$ and the multiplier
weight $\mcl V$ are synthesized simultaneously. In the primal IQC
condition, $\mcl V$ weights the quadratic expression in the filtered
closed-loop signals. Since these signals depend on $K$, this expression
couples the controller gains and the multiplier weight nonconvexly.
The controller-dependent output rows in
\eqref{eq:primal-system-components} display this dependence explicitly;
using the $J$-spectral factor $\Theta$ fixes the weight to
$J_{\mbf n_z,\mbf n_w}$, but does not remove the first obstacle.
Joint synthesis of $K$ and $\mcl V$ is therefore generally nonconvex
in the original primal variables, even for ODE systems, where the
reconstruction operators are identities. Obtaining convex synthesis
conditions requires additional structure or a suitable
reformulation~\cite{scherer2009robust,7353149}.
For general dynamic multipliers, established synthesis methods alternate
between controller design and multiplier
analysis~\cite{veenman2014iqcsynthesis,wang2016robustiqc}.

These obstacles motivate the dual formulation developed next. By changing
the placement of the controller in the component realization, it enables
the alternative change of variables used for controller synthesis.
