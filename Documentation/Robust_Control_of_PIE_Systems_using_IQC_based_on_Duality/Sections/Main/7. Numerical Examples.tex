We consider PDE examples with repeated parametric and sector-bounded
uncertainties.

\subsection{Note on loop shifting with $\mcl J$}

In our examples we perform the loop shift $\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
\circ\Delta$ on distributed uncertainty channels entering the dynamics.
The effect of this loop shift can be seen from
$\dot{\mbf v}=\mbf v_{ss}+\delta^2\mbf v$, with $\mbf v(t,0)=0$ and
$|\delta|\leq\alpha$. Representing $\delta^2$ by two repeated channels and
spatially differentiating their inputs and outputs gives
\begin{align*}
 \dot{\mbf v}&=\mbf v_{ss}+\mcl Jw_{\Delta,2},\\
 z_{\Delta,1}&=\mbf v_s,\qquad z_{\Delta,2}=w_{\Delta,1},\\
 w_{\Delta,i}&=\delta z_{\Delta,i},\qquad i\in\{1,2\}.
\end{align*}
Define the spatial integration operator $\mcl J\in\Pi_3$ by
$(\mcl Jf)(t,s):=\int_0^s f(t,\theta)\,d\theta$.
Closing the loop recovers
$\mcl Jw_{\Delta,2}=\delta^2\mcl J\mbf v_s=\delta^2\mbf v$. The
feedthrough $z_{\Delta,2}=w_{\Delta,1}$ remains unfiltered, since filtering
it would instead produce the different term $\delta^2\mcl J\mbf v$. This
realization substantially improves feasibility in our computations,
possibly because it better aligns with the fundamental PIE state. We do not
have a general analytical explanation and therefore treat this benefit as
empirical and realization- and uncertainty-dependent.

We begin with the finite-dimensional ODE benchmark of
Veenman et al.~\cite[Sec.~8.1]{veenman2016robust}. We copy its temporal filter basis, 
and performance setup, and apply the same
construction to a pair of coupled heat equations by lifting the filter over the 
spatial domain. For the static part we can use general PI operators. This multiplier
setup aligns with the multiplier class in \cite[Lem.~10]{10156335}. We use
the same finite-dimensional matrices as in the ODE benchmark, but apply them on
distributed signals, and add a diffusion
diffusion to obtain the coupled PDE. Let $s\in[0,1]$ and
\begin{align*}
 A&=\bmat{-2&-3\\1&1}+\frac{\pi^2}{2}I,
 &B_\Delta&=\bmat{1&0\\0&0},&B_{\mrm p}&=\bmat{1\\0},\\
 C_\Delta&=\bmat{1&0\\0&0},
 &D_{\Delta}&=\bmat{1&-2\\1&-1},
 &D_{\Delta\mrm p}&=\bmat{0\\1},\\
 C_{\mrm p}&=\bmat{1&0},
 &D_{\mrm p\Delta}&=\bmat{0&1}.
\end{align*}
Consider
\begin{align*}
 \dot{\mbf v}&=\frac{1}{10}\mbf v_{ss}+A\mbf v
      +B_\Delta\mcl Jw_\Delta+sB_{\mrm p}w_{\mrm p},
 &\mbf v(t,0)&=0,\quad \mbf v_s(t,1)=0,\\
 z_\Delta&=C_\Delta\mbf v_s+D_{\Delta\Delta}w_\Delta
      +D_{\Delta\mrm p}w_{\mrm p},
 &w_\Delta&=\delta z_\Delta,\quad |\delta|\leq\alpha,\\
 z_{\mrm p}&=3\int_0^1s\left(C_{\mrm p}\mbf v
      +D_{\mrm p\Delta}\mcl Jw_\Delta\right)ds.&&
\end{align*}
The same real scalar $\delta$ is repeated over both channels and all spatial
locations, and $\Delta=\delta I$ is self-adjoint. We therefore use the same
multiplier family in the two analyses,
\begin{align*}
 &\mcl V_\Delta\{\alpha^2\mcl Q_\Delta,
 \alpha\mcl S_\Delta,-\mcl Q_\Delta\},\qquad
 \mcl Q_\Delta\succeq0,\quad \mcl S_\Delta^*=-\mcl S_\Delta, \\
&\underline{\mcl V}_\Delta\{\alpha^2\underline{\mcl Q}_\Delta,
 \alpha\underline{\mcl S}_\Delta,-\underline{\mcl Q}_\Delta\},\qquad
 \underline{\mcl Q}_\Delta\succeq0,\quad \underline{\mcl S}_\Delta^*=-\underline{\mcl S}_\Delta.
\end{align*}
This is the situation
described in Section~\ref{sec:dynamic-filtering-duality}: because
$\Delta=\Delta^*$, the primal and dual certificates can use the same dynamic
filter and repeated-real IQC structure, with separate decision operators on
the two sides.

The dynamic uncertainty filter is constructed from the stable temporal basis
\begin{equation*}
 \psi_\nu(p)=\bmat{1&\dfrac{-\rho}{p-\rho}&\cdots&
 \left(\dfrac{-\rho}{p-\rho}\right)^\nu}^{\!T},
 \qquad \rho<0,
\end{equation*}
where $p$ denotes the Laplace variable. To lift this finite-dimensional
filter to the spatial signal space, its state-space matrices are placed in
the pointwise $R_0$ blocks of the corresponding PI operators, while the
integral kernels $R_1$ and $R_2$ are set to zero. Thus the same temporal
filter acts independently at every $s\in[0,1]$; identical lifted copies are
used on the $z_\Delta$ and $w_\Delta$ channels. The gain from $w_{\mrm p}$
to $z_{\mrm p}$ is tested with the primal and dual performance multipliers
$\mcl V_{\mrm p}\{1,0,-\gamma^2\}$ and
$\underline{\mcl V}_{\mrm p}\{1,0,-\underline\gamma^2\}$, respectively.

For each uncertainty radius and filter order, the primal and dual LPIs are
solved on the same pole grid. Figure~\ref{fig:coupled-heat-primal-dual}
shows their optimized gains and pointwise difference. The computation times
for the individual filter orders are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:coupled-heat-timing}.

\subsection{Boundary control with sector-bounded uncertainty}
\label{ex:sector-pdes}

The following dual IQC is used for both nonlinear PDEs below.
\begin{lemma}[Dual sector-bound IQC]\label{lem:sec-bound}
Suppose that $(\Delta z_\Delta)(t,s)=\phi(z_\Delta(t,s))$ and
\begin{equation*}
 \alpha z^2\leq z\phi(z)\leq\beta z^2,
 \qquad z\in\R,
\end{equation*}
where $\alpha<\beta$. Then $\Delta$ satisfies the dual hard IQC with
$\underline\Psi_\Delta=I$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dual-sec-mult}
 \underline{\mcl V}_\Delta=
 \bmat{\beta&-1\\-\alpha&1}^{*}
 \bmat{0&\underline{\mcl S}_\Delta\\
       \underline{\mcl S}_\Delta&0}
 \bmat{\beta&-1\\-\alpha&1},
\end{equation}
where $\underline{\mcl S}_\Delta\in\Pi_3$ is the positive multiplication
operator
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dual-sec-par}
 (\underline{\mcl S}_\Delta\mbf x)(t,s)
 =\underline S_0(s)\mbf x(t,s),\qquad \underline S_0(s)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
See~\ref{proof:sec-bound}.
\end{proof}

Optimal and robust boundary control of the sine--Gordon equation has also
been studied on finite horizons~\cite{petcu2004control}.

Consider boundary control of the reaction--diffusion (RD) and damped wave
(W) equations
\begin{align*}
 \dot{\mbf v}_{\mrm{RD}}
   &=\mbf v_{\mrm{RD},ss}
     +\lambda_{\mrm{RD}}\sin(\mbf v_{\mrm{RD}})+s w_{\mrm p},\\
 \ddot{\mbf v}_{\mrm W}
   &=\mbf v_{\mrm W,ss}-2\dot{\mbf v}_{\mrm W}
     +\lambda_{\mrm W}\sin(\mbf v_{\mrm W})+s w_{\mrm p},
\end{align*}
with, for $i\in\{\mrm{RD},\mrm W\}$,
\begin{equation*}
 \mbf v_i(t,0)=0,\qquad \mbf v_{i,s}(t,1)=x_i(t),\qquad
 \dot x_i(t)=u_i(t),\qquad
 z_{\mrm p}=\bmat{x_i&\int_0^1\mbf v_i(t,s)ds}^{\!T}.
\end{equation*}
For synthesis, set
\begin{equation*}
 z_{\Delta,i}=\mbf v_{i,s},\qquad
 w_{\Delta,i}=\cos(\mcl Jz_{\Delta,i})z_{\Delta,i},
\end{equation*}
and replace $\lambda_i\sin(\mbf v_i)$ by
$\lambda_i\mcl Jw_{\Delta,i}$. Since $\mcl Jz_{\Delta,i}=\mbf v_i$,
this is an equivalent realization; explicitly,
\begin{equation*}
 \mcl Jw_{\Delta,i}
 =\mcl J\!\left(\cos(\mbf v_i)\mbf v_{i,s}\right)
 =\sin(\mbf v_i)-\sin(\mbf v_i(t,0))=\sin(\mbf v_i).
\end{equation*}
Also, $s w_{\mrm p}(t)=(\mcl Jw_{\mrm p})(t,s)$. Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
 -z_{\Delta,i}^2\leq z_{\Delta,i}w_{\Delta,i}
 \leq z_{\Delta,i}^2,
\end{equation*}
so the same $[-1,1]$ sector IQC applies to both plants. We use
Lemma~\ref{lem:sec-bound} with $\alpha=-1$, $\beta=1$, the performance
multiplier $\underline{\mcl V}_{\mrm p}\{1,0,-\gamma^2I_2\}$, and
$\|\mcl K_i\|\leq10^4$. As discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:dynamic-filtering-duality}, the primal and dual analyses
use the same sector-IQC structure, while the synthesis condition uses its
underlined dual counterpart.

The computations use $\lambda_{\mrm{RD}}=5$ and $\lambda_{\mrm W}=3$. The
state simulations use
$w_{\mrm p}(t)=8\cos(t)\mathbf 1_{[\pi,4\pi]}(t)$. For the separately
reported wave certificate, the performance output is specialized to
$z_{\mrm p}(t)=\int_0^1\mbf v_{\mrm W}(t,s)ds$.

For this scalar wave objective, solving \eqref{eq:controller-kyp} yields
the certified bound $\gamma=0.45$ for the
closed-loop interconnection $P_K\star\Delta$. The corresponding
state-feedback controller is
\begin{equation*}
 u(t)=-15.46x(t)+\int_0^1\left(
 2Q_1(s)\mbf v_{\mrm W,ss}(t,s)
 +Q_2(s)\dot{\mbf v}_{\mrm W}(t,s)\right)ds,
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{align*}
 Q_1(s)={}&200.61s^{12}-1537.17s^{11}+5033.27s^{10}-9096.06s^9\\
 &+9933.67s^8-6788.31s^7+2916.66s^6-700.58s^5\\
 &-82.97s^4+161.05s^3-40.98s^2+3.72s-7.6113,\\
 Q_2(s)={}&203.29s^{12}-1309.04s^{11}+3721.08s^{10}-5890.06s^9\\
 &+5519.18s^8-3072.06s^7+942.76s^6+1.51s^5\\
 &-274.86s^4+166.68s^3-21.49s^2+11.76s-0.07.
\end{align*}

For these parameter values, the dual synthesis LPI produces stabilizing
boundary controllers. Figure~\ref{fig:sector-pde-states} shows the resulting
open- and closed-loop responses.
