A recent state-space framework based on Partial Integral Equations (PIEs)
provides a formulation of stabilization and
$H_\infty$-optimal state-feedback controller synthesis for PDEs
\cite{shivaknew}. This framework addresses the nominal setting, corresponding
to $\Delta=0$. Building on this result, we develop a framework for
optimal state-feedback control of uncertain or nonlinear PIEs subject to a
prescribed quadratic performance requirement. Let $G$ denote the open-loop
PIE plant to which the controller is connected. For a state-feedback controller $K$, the closed-loop
interconnection $K\star G\star\Delta$ has the realization
\begin{gather*}
    \bmat{\mcl{T}\dot{\mbf{x}}(t)\\z(t)} = \bmat{\mcl{A}+\mcl{B}_\mrm{u}\mcl{K} & \mcl{B}\\
    \mcl{C}+\mcl{D}_\mrm{zu}\mcl{K}& \mcl{D}}\bmat{\mbf{x}(t) \\ w(t)} \\
    w_\Delta = \Delta z_\Delta
\end{gather*}
where $z(t)=\bmat{z_\Delta(t)\\z_\mrm{p}(t)}$, and $w(t) = \bmat{w_\Delta(t) \\ w_\mrm{P}(t)}$.
Our objective is to construct a state-feedback controller $K$ such that
$K\star G\star\Delta$ is robustly stable and satisfies the prescribed
performance requirement for every $\Delta\in\mbf\Delta$.
Throughout the paper, we make the following \emph{standing assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A.\arabic*),ref=A.\arabic*,leftmargin=*]
\item\label{ass:nominal-feasibility}
There exists a state-feedback controller $K$ such that the nominal
closed loop $K\star G$ is stable and satisfies the prescribed performance
requirement.
\item\label{ass:well-posedness}
$K\star G\star\Delta$ is well-posed for every
$\Delta\in\mbf\Delta$.
\item\label{ass:initial-conditions}
Robust stability and performance are analyzed with $\mbf x(0)=0$.
\item\label{ass:signal-availability}
The controller has access to the PIE state $\mbf x$. For a dynamic-filter
controller, $\Delta$, $z_\Delta$, $w_\Delta=\Delta z_\Delta$, and
$w_{\mrm p}$ are also available if filtered.
\item\label{ass:boundary-channels}
Exogenous inputs and uncertainty do not act directly on the boundary
\cite[Sec.~III-B]{shivaknew}. Boundary channels satisfy
$\hat w=\dot w$, where $w$ is available for state reconstruction. For boundary
control, $\dot u$ is generated by the controller and
$u(t)=u(0)+\int_0^t\dot u(\tau)\,\mrm d\tau$ is the actual input to the plant.
\end{enumerate}

performance.
