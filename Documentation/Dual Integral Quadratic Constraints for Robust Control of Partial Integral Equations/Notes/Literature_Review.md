# Literature review for robust controller synthesis using PIE and IQC duality

The strongest positioning for this manuscript is a synthesis result at the intersection of partial integral equation (PIE) duality and integral quadratic constraint (IQC) theory. The individual ingredients already have substantial literatures. A convincing contribution would establish precisely when dynamically filtered IQC certificates transfer through PIE duality and how that transfer produces implementable controllers through convex linear PI inequalities (LPIs).

This assessment concerns the manuscript headed *Robust Controller Synthesis*, particularly Sections III–VIII and the duality proofs. It covers foundational literature and relevant publications available through 10 September 2026. It is a focused scholarly review, not an exhaustive bibliometric census or a proof of priority. The source inventory distinguishes papers read in full or in relevant technical sections from records used only for abstract-level positioning. Recommendations and mathematical observations about the manuscript are analytical judgments, not claims that the cited authors assessed this manuscript.

**1. Principal findings**

Three comparisons should organize the introduction. First, PIE duality already supports nominal optimal state-feedback synthesis. Second, infinite-dimensional IQCs already support robust analysis, with subsequent work addressing structured robustness and observer synthesis. Third, IQC duality already supports particular finite-dimensional convex synthesis problems. The paper should explain the additional hypotheses and operator constructions needed to combine these results for robust feedback synthesis. [PIE duality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.13104v4), [infinite-dimensional IQCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10387136/), [earlier PIE robust synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14896v1), [LPV IQC duality](https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~SeilerControl/Papers/2018/VenkataramanEtAl_18IJRNC_ConvexLPVSynthesisOfEstimatorsAndFeedforwards.pdf).

The most consequential missing connection is the literature on **doubly-hard factorizations and finite-horizon IQCs with terminal costs**. These works address the precise transition from frequency-domain multipliers to truncated time-domain statements on both sides of an interconnection. They belong beside the paper's main duality theorem. [Carrasco and Seiler](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04816), [Scherer and Veenman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05804).

The intended convexity claim should specify what is fixed: multiplier filter, factorization, uncertainty description, controller information pattern, and polynomial certificate degree. A convex controller-design subproblem with a fixed factor is materially different from a joint optimization over controller and multiplier dynamics. Established iterative IQC synthesis provides the relevant comparison. [Veenman and Scherer](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rnc.3042), [Wang, Pfifer, and Seiler](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109816000546).

Several bibliography entries already represent the right literature but are not integrated into the argument. The main needs are selective explanation and precise citation placement, together with additions on truncation, general IQC synthesis, and alternative PDE-control methods.

**2. Dissipativity and the IQC framework**

Willems's dissipativity theory supplies the storage-versus-supply interpretation underlying the paper's operator inequalities. Its relevance is conceptual: integrating a differential storage inequality gives a finite-time bound with a terminal storage term. This is the appropriate starting point for explaining why positivity, initial conditions, and terminal terms matter. It should not be cited as an infinite-dimensional PIE KYP theorem. [1: Willems](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00276493).

Megretski and Rantzer organize uncertainty and nonlinear component properties into IQCs and combine them with a nominal-system inequality. Their work is the foundational reference for the framework, whereas particular hard time-domain statements need their own hypotheses. The manuscript's introduction and first IQC definition should cite this foundation explicitly; its presence only in the bibliography is insufficient. [2: Megretski and Rantzer](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/publication/8311510).

Veenman, Scherer, and Köroğlu provide a broad computational treatment of robust stability and performance using IQCs. This is useful for introducing performance channels and multiplier parameterizations, and for situating an LPI formulation relative to established LMI analysis. General quadratic performance and a joint stability/performance certificate are established themes, so the new contribution must be their PIE-duality synthesis implementation. [3: Veenman et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0947358016300097).

Seiler's dissipation-based analysis identifies factorization conditions that support finite-horizon IQCs and appropriate nonnegative storage. For the rational positive-negative setting, a suitable J-spectral factor connects the frequency and time-domain tests. The manuscript should distinguish this result from the much stronger assertion that two factorizations produce identical truncated quadratic integrals for every input. [4: Seiler](https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~SeilerControl/Papers/2015/Seiler_15TAC_StabilityAnalysisWithDIandIQCs.pdf).

Pfifer and Seiler extend dissipation/IQC reasoning to gridded LPV plants and worst-case input-output gain. Their 2015 paper also gives a concrete factorization construction useful for the manuscript's finite-dimensional multiplier implementation. This establishes both a conceptual precedent and an algorithmic reference; the Riccati computation is supporting machinery, not a new contribution. [5: Pfifer and Seiler](https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~SeilerControl/Papers/2015/PfiferSeiler_15IJRNC_RobustnessAnalysisOfLPVSystemsUsingIQCs.pdf).

Their subsequent work allows more general storage descriptions by accounting for energy associated with the multiplier. Hu, Lacerda, and Seiler develop related discrete-time results. These references explain why imposing an elementary nonnegative augmented storage can be restrictive, but neither automatically proves an operator-valued continuous-time extension. Cite them as motivation and identify which assumptions the PIE result retains. [6: Pfifer and Seiler](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1116029/less-conservative-robustness-analysis-of-linear-parameter-varying-systems-using-integral-quadratic-constraints), [7: Hu et al.](https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~SeilerControl/Papers/2017/HuEtAl_17IJRNC_RobustnessAnalysisOfUncertainDiscreteTimeSystems.pdf).

Carrasco and Seiler distinguish hard from doubly-hard factorizations: preserving the relevant finite-horizon inequality for one graph is not enough to transfer both graph and inverse-graph conditions. Their examples make this distinction concrete. Scherer and Veenman formulate finite-horizon IQCs with a terminal cost, providing another route from frequency-domain analysis to time-domain certificates. These are complementary references for repairing or narrowing the factorization step. [8: Carrasco and Seiler](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04816), [9: Scherer and Veenman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05804).

Scherer's later survey provides a useful unifying account of dissipativity, multipliers, and computational robustness tests. It is suitable for orientation and identifying further references, while the manuscript should still attribute specific theorems to their original papers. [10: Scherer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07401).

**3. IQC duality and controller synthesis**

The estimation/feedforward literature is especially close. Scherer and Köse's 2008 work concerns nominal-stability characterization and robust estimation using dynamic IQCs. Köse and Scherer's 2009 paper uses dual IQC constraints to obtain convex disturbance-feedforward synthesis. Their control problem and uncertainty assumptions should be compared explicitly with the manuscript's feedback problem, rather than grouped under an undifferentiated claim about robust control. [11: Scherer and Köse](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/robustness-with-dynamic-iqcs-an-exact-state-space-characterizatio/), [12: Köse and Scherer](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rnc.1374).

Venkataraman and Seiler are the closest methodological predecessor. Their Section 3 constructs dual multipliers and factors, relates an uncertainty-weighted analysis to a bounded-real problem, and handles changes of factorization through storage transformations. Their synthesis applications are output estimation and disturbance feedforward for gridded LPV systems. Compare specifically with equations (17)–(18) and Lemmas 3–6 in the author manuscript. [13: Venkataraman and Seiler](https://dept.aem.umn.edu/~SeilerControl/Papers/2018/VenkataramanEtAl_18IJRNC_ConvexLPVSynthesisOfEstimatorsAndFeedforwards.pdf).

The manuscript's proposed advance is an operator realization and feedback synthesis construction. Merely obtaining the same algebraic dual-filter expression is not enough to distinguish it. The comparison should identify the noncommuting reconstruction operator, admissible inverses, signal spaces, and controller recovery. The formulation should also explain how the signal-level proof relates to storage-level reasoning without asserting equivalence of restricted certificate searches that has not been established.

Scherer's work on systems without control-channel uncertainties is an essential boundary on broad convexity claims. It identifies a structural setting encompassing important convex synthesis problems. The present paper needs an equally explicit description of its admissible plant/controller structure. Full-state feedback and access to uncertainty-channel signals should appear in the problem statement, not emerge only during controller recovery. [14: Scherer](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/robust-controller-synthesis-is-convex-for-systems-without-control).

For general dynamic multipliers, Veenman and Scherer alternate nominal synthesis and IQC analysis. Wang, Pfifer, and Seiler develop a related coordinate-descent method for uncertain gridded LPV plants. These are stronger comparisons than a generic remark that robust synthesis is difficult: they show exactly where a fixed-multiplier convex subproblem fits into an overall nonconvex procedure. [15: Veenman and Scherer](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rnc.3042), [16: Wang et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109816000546).

Yuan and Wu develop exact-memory control for uncertain LFT systems with time-varying state delay, with convex LMI conditions including multiplier scalings in their specified construction. This is a direct prior example of dynamic-IQC-based feedback synthesis. It deserves a substantive comparison covering the controller's memory, uncertainty structure, and available signals. Its abstract supports the synthesis comparison; detailed measurement assumptions should be checked in the full paper before being attributed verbatim. [17: Yuan and Wu](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26685278/).

The March 2026 preprint by Zhang and Wu combines IQCs for saturation and structured uncertainty, using a controller that feeds back the dead-zone output. It reinforces that auxiliary nonlinear-channel feedback is a meaningful part of the information pattern. It is relevant recent context, but its finite-dimensional construction does not settle the PIE operator problem. Label it as a preprint. [18: Zhang and Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07907v1).

Two other recent developments help delimit scope. Schwenkel and coauthors use iterative discrete-time synthesis for multiple performance measures, including peak bounds, with finite-horizon terminal-cost IQCs. Gupta, Klauser, and Karimi use nonparametric IQCs in data-driven MIMO controller synthesis. Neither is a direct replacement for continuous-time PIE feedback design; both make broad claims of first IQC synthesis or first multiobjective IQC design untenable. [19: Schwenkel et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rnc.70098), [20: Gupta et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2025.112608).

**4. PIE representations, robust analysis, and dual synthesis**

Peet's PIE representation replaces spatial differentiation and implicit boundary restrictions with a bounded-operator description for a specified PDE class. This is the natural foundational citation for the preliminaries. The paper should distinguish exact conversion of the plant equations from finite-dimensional parameterization of the certificate search. The former does not make the latter exhaustive. [21: Peet](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10285388).

The GPDE input-output extension adds support for more general derivative orders, boundary inputs, and ODE coupling. Cite it where the admissible PDE class and boundary-control realization are defined. Its role is to justify the model conversion, rather than the subsequent IQC duality. [22: Shivakumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03735).

Shivakumar, Das, and Peet establish PIE dual representations and nominal optimal feedback constructions. Their current arXiv version contains the cited Theorem 9, while an older author PDF has different numbering. The current theorem explicitly uses finite-dimensional external input/output channels; Corollary 8 describes decay of reconstructed states. These details matter when restating the results for spatial uncertainty channels. [23: current author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.13104v4).

Das and coauthors introduce PI multipliers for robust ODE–PDE analysis. Talitckii and coauthors subsequently formulate hard IQCs with infinite-dimensional channels and a sufficient PIE KYP test. These already cover the infinite-dimensional robustness-analysis foundation. Their assumptions should be carried into the new synthesis statement, particularly well-posedness, boundedness, and the role of the filter. [24: Das et al.](https://control.asu.edu/Publications/2020/Das_CDC_2020.pdf), [25: Talitckii et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10387136/).

The 2025 Lenssen–Talitckii–Peet–Das preprint is the closest same-framework predecessor. Its stated contributions include joint robust stability/performance LPIs, connections to structured singular-value bounds, and robust Luenberger observer synthesis. The present introduction should state explicitly that the additional target is feedback controller synthesis through IQC duality. A claim to introduce robust synthesis for PIEs without qualification would obscure this prior contribution. [26: Lenssen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14896v1).

Peet's delay-representation work is directly relevant to the delayed firing-rate example. It supports the conversion among DDE, DDF, ODE–PDE, and PIE forms. Known delays represented as transport states should be distinguished from uncertain delays modeled by IQCs; they lead to different uncertainty descriptions and different controller information requirements. [27: Peet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03881).

PIETOOLS documentation provides the computational context for polynomial operators, conversion, synthesis, and simulation. The current arXiv record is titled *PIETOOLS 2025: User Manual*, revised in March 2026; the original version had a different release title. Record the software release and solver actually used in the numerical examples. The existence of a newer manual is not evidence that the computations used that release. [28: Shivakumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17854v2).

**5. Multiplier families and spectral factorization**

Zames and Falb provide the classical basis for exploiting monotonicity and slope restriction beyond a sector bound. Carrasco, Turner, and Heath review continuous-time multiplier searches and their limitations. Turner gives an accessible derivation useful for checking the sign and norm restrictions on the impulse response. The manuscript should retain the distinction between sector-bounded and slope-restricted nonlinearities, and between odd and general nonlinearities. [29: Zames and Falb](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0306007), [30: Carrasco et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0947358015001119), [31: Turner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15913).

For the firing-rate example, an equilibrium shift preserves the derivative bound of the sigmoid but generally destroys oddness. Thus an odd-nonlinearity multiplier cannot be justified merely because the midpoint-centered sigmoid is a hyperbolic tangent. The current use of a construction that does not require oddness is the appropriate direction. Keep the admissible impulse-response restrictions explicit and use the same nonlinear function in the mathematical certificate and the simulation.

Meinsma is the reference for the rational J-spectral factorization and equalizing-vector connection used in the Riccati construction. Green, Glover, Limebeer, and Doyle establish the older role of J-spectral factorization in H-infinity control. These references help attribute the computational machinery correctly; neither should be presented as a direct theorem on polynomial PI-valued multiplier factors. [32: Meinsma](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0167691194000779), [33: Green et al.](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/ken8c-f3h13/latest).

A particularly useful addition is Iftime and Zwart's *J-spectral factorization and equalizing vectors*. Despite sharing Meinsma's title, it is a different paper. It discusses Wiener-class matrix-valued functions, Toeplitz invertibility, and connections to infinite-dimensional system classes. It can guide further operator-factorization work, but matrix-valued external channels must not be confused with arbitrary infinite-dimensional multiplier channels or closure inside a polynomial PI algebra. [34: Iftime and Zwart](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/j-spectral-factorization-and-equalizing-vectors/).

A practical presentation should state which of the following is actually established: a static PI weight with an explicitly admissible factor; a rational temporal filter acting on finite-dimensional channels; or a fully operator-valued dynamic multiplier. A theorem covering the first two cases is already useful. Claiming the third requires separate existence, inverse, realization, and truncation results.

**6. Alternative PDE-control approaches and example-specific literature**

PDE backstepping is an essential comparison for boundary feedback. Krstić and Smyshlyaev provide a systematic treatment based on transformations to stable target PDEs. The comparison should concern admissible equations, measurements, uncertainty classes, performance criteria, and computation. A blanket statement that PDE control lacks systematic methods would be misleading. [35: Krstić and Smyshlyaev](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/1.9780898718607.fm).

Direct Lyapunov and LMI methods also predate PIE synthesis. Fridman and Blighovsky design sampled-data controllers for uncertain semilinear parabolic systems using finitely many spatial measurements. Valmorbida, Ahmadi, and Papachristodoulou use semidefinite programming for PDE stability analysis. These are useful comparators for the practical information pattern and for the distinction between PDE-level certification and certificate parameterization. [36: Fridman and Blighovsky](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/robust-sampled-data-control-of-a-class-of-semilinear-parabolic-sy/), [37: Valmorbida et al.](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A3418aab4-0c9c-4fc6-a366-777efe63945e/files/m55630ddd1d5e9c3ad1aa3ba1e6223df9).

The sine–Gordon example should cite control literature for that equation. Petcu and Temam study finite-horizon optimal and robust boundary control, including an existence/uniqueness formulation. This is prior robust control of the example class, but it is a different objective and solution framework from an infinite-horizon induced-gain LPI certificate. The comparison should emphasize the certificate, actuator, and uncertainty description rather than claim a first controller for sine–Gordon dynamics. [38: Petcu and Temam](https://www.numdam.org/item/COCV_2004__10_4_553_0/).

Nevado Holgado, Terry, and Bogacz supply the STN–GPe firing-rate model and its oscillation context. Their model is a source for the example dynamics, not validation of the added stimulation architecture or a physiological guarantee for the proposed controller. State which parameters and equilibrium are used, and separate mathematical attenuation of model outputs from claims about suppressing a particular frequency band. [39: Nevado Holgado et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20844130/).

|---|---|---|
| Nominal PIE feedback | Same plant, no uncertainty-aware design | Reanalysis under exactly the proposed uncertainty set |
| Static versus dynamic IQCs | Added value of slope information and filter memory | Same objective, measurements, margins, and certificate degree |

**7. Technical implications for the manuscript's literature claims**

The following points affect how strongly the paper can claim to extend the cited results. They are not a complete correctness review.

**Finite-horizon factorization equality.** Definition `def:factorization` requires equality between the quadratic forms of `(Psi,V)` and `(Theta,J)` after truncating their outputs, for every horizon. Standard frequency-domain factorization equality does not establish that definition. The difficulty persists even with stable, stably invertible rational factors.

An elementary illustration can be embedded in a strict positive-negative multiplier. Let the temporal transfer variable be `s`, set

\[
\Psi(s)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\(s+1)^{-1}&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
V=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1),\qquad
\Theta(s)=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{s+\sqrt2}{s+1},1\right).
\]

Then `Psi~ V Psi = Theta~ diag(1,-1) Theta`. Both diagonal blocks of the multiplier have the required strict signs on the imaginary axis. Nevertheless, with second input zero and first input equal to one on `[0,T]`, the filter state is `x(t)=1-exp(-t)` and direct integration gives

\[
\int_0^T\!\left[(\Psi u)^T V(\Psi u)
 -(\Theta u)^T J(\Theta u)\right]dt
=-(\sqrt2-1)(1-e^{-T})^2.
\]

This is nonzero for `T>0`. The calculation is an independent observation about the manuscript's definition. A repair can start directly from the admissible hard factor `(Theta,J)`, prove the needed graph/inverse-graph implications, or carry the terminal storage correction. It cannot simply replace an arbitrary factorization while preserving all truncated integrals.

**Uniform strictness.** In `thm:dual-iqc`, pointwise strict contraction should be replaced by a uniform bound with some `rho<1`. Strict inequality cannot hold for the zero signal or a zero horizon, and strict contraction for every nonzero vector does not imply an operator norm strictly below one in infinite dimensions. The proof already assumes a uniform `rho`, so the theorem should match it.

**Hypotheses used by the dual-filter proof.** The proof uses bounded causal inverses of `Theta`, `Theta_22`, and `Gamma = Theta_21 P + Theta_22`. List these hypotheses or prove them from stated assumptions. Invertible feedthrough alone does not establish a stable causal inverse of the dynamic map. Explain why the normalized system `G_0` remains in the system class to which gain duality is applied.

**Channel-space extension.** The current source for `shivaknew`, Theorem 9, uses finite-dimensional external channels. Restating it with Hilbert-valued input and output signals is an extension. A convolution/duality proof may generalize, but the argument should be supplied, including solution regularity and the required integrability. Cite the original theorem and clearly label the generalized statement. This is potentially part of the paper's contribution.

**State norms and stability.** Preserve the distinction between the fundamental PIE state `x` and reconstructed state `T x`. If the reconstruction inverse is unbounded in the ambient norm, decay of the latter is not automatically decay of the former. The statement invoking Corollary 8 should specify the actual norm and state quantity controlled. Likewise, zero-initial-condition input-output performance needs additional reasoning to imply an internal-state stability claim.

**Certificate feasibility.** The opening of Section VI says that primal–dual equivalence preserves feasibility. The current KYP argument establishes sufficiency of either LPI and transfers signal properties; it does not by itself establish a converse between existence of polynomial PI storage certificates. Inverting or transforming a certificate may leave its chosen degree or polynomial class. State the level of equivalence: signal inequalities, unrestricted bounded operators, PI operators, or a fixed SDP parameterization.

**Polynomial inverse closure.** Positive polynomial multiplication by `2+s` on a bounded spatial interval has bounded inverse multiplication by `1/(2+s)`, which is not polynomial. Thus even a simple coercive operator need not have its inverse in the polynomial subclass defined in the preliminaries. Controller recovery can still be valid in a broader bounded-operator class, but exact realizability, approximation, and verification must be explained separately.

**Convexity and performance scaling.** Equation `eq:controller-kyp` is affine in `P,Z` when its other blocks are fixed. If those blocks depend on a variable spectral factor, the displayed affine structure does not establish joint convexity. When optimizing `gamma`, show whether the chosen normalization gives an affine dependence on `gamma` or `gamma^2`, or use a stated feasibility search. This distinction is particularly relevant to the dynamic-multiplier example.

**Controller information.** The dynamic realization assumes access to plant state, uncertainty-channel signals, and in one statement the performance disturbance. Specify which signals are measured, reconstructed from known nonlinearities, or unavailable under genuine parametric uncertainty. A known nonlinearity and an unknown uncertainty are different implementation cases. Certification through a dual IQC does not itself supply missing measurements.

**Nonlinearity lifting.** In the sine–Gordon example, the lifted map is spatially nonlocal: `Delta_2 z(s)=cos(integral_0^s z) z(s)`. Its pointwise sector inequality follows from `|cos|<=1`, but the stated sector lemma assumes the form `phi(z(s))`. Extend the lemma to the needed pointwise sector relation or explain why its proof uses only that relation. Do not automatically transfer the original sine function's scalar slope restriction to the lifted spatial operator.

**Local sigmoid bounds.** A slope bound below the global maximum applies only on a restricted input interval. The current manuscript recognizes that an induced L2 bound alone does not prove invariance of that interval. Retain this qualification and supply an invariant-set or pointwise-bound argument if the numerical claims depend on local slopes. Terminal-cost and peak-performance IQC methods suggest directions, but their discrete-time or finite-dimensional results require a new PIE derivation before use.

**8. Recommended contribution language and citation placement**

A defensible contribution statement, conditional on completing the stated technical steps, is:

> We develop a duality-based robust state-feedback synthesis framework for uncertain systems admitting a PIE representation. For admissible hard IQC factors with the required causal inverse and realization properties, the construction transfers a primal performance inequality to a dual PIE and yields sufficient controller-synthesis LPIs. Static factors give state-feedback controllers, while dynamic factors introduce controller states driven by specified available signals. The resulting optimization is convex for fixed filter data and the stated certificate parameterization.

Avoid describing the result as unrestricted convex robust synthesis, a general infinite-dimensional spectral factorization theorem, or lossless equivalence of finite-degree SDPs unless those stronger statements are proved. None is necessary for a useful paper.

**9. Numerical comparisons most likely to strengthen the paper**

The first priority is a matched comparison between nominal PIE feedback and uncertainty-aware synthesis, followed by static and dynamic uncertainty multipliers. Use the same plant, uncertainty radius, measured signals, performance outputs, controller-gain restrictions, solver tolerances, and certificate-degree choices. Otherwise a better bound cannot be attributed specifically to dynamic IQC duality.

The reaction–diffusion example reports synthesis and reanalysis gains of 0.798, 0.743, and 0.748. Explain which optimizations were rerun and whether their certificate classes differ. A small primal/dual numerical gap is consistent with several possibilities, including parameterization and numerical tolerances; the numbers alone neither prove nor refute unrestricted duality.

For the dynamic example, give at least one explicit admissible multiplier, the resulting factor, the causal controller realization, and a reproducible synthesis result. Report factorization residuals, stability/inverse margins, polynomial degrees, solver status, and computation time. A mathematical dynamic-filter framework needs a completed dynamic-filter example to demonstrate its advantage over the static case.

For each recovered controller, distinguish synthesis certificates from simulation evidence. Simulated trajectories do not establish robustness over the entire uncertainty class. Conversely, a conservative IQC certificate can remain valuable even if the simulations show much better performance. Include the gain norm's state space; a gain on a highest-derivative PIE state has a different measurement interpretation from one on the physical PDE displacement.

If comparison with a discretized controller is added, include spatial-resolution refinement and an argument controlling the residual modes before making a full-PDE guarantee. If an alternating multiplier search is used, record the outer iteration and initialization. Optimization infeasibility should be reported as failure of the chosen certificate search, not proof that robust stabilization is impossible.
