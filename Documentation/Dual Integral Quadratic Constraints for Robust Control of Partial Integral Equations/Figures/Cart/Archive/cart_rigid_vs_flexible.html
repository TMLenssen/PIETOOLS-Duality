Model, assumptions and interpretation

The cart carries a slender upright frame. On the left the frame is perfectly rigid; on the right it bends elastically. Both bases follow exactly the same position-servo dynamics: q″ + 2ωc q′ + ωc²(q − r) = 0. The reference changes by 150 mm. At t = 9 s the servo response parameter ωc increases from 1.6 to 10 rad/s. This parameter is the closed-loop natural frequency, not the exact −3 dB bandwidth.

The flexible frame is a uniform clamped-free Euler–Bernoulli beam driven by base acceleration: ρA(w_tt + q″) + EI w_ssss = 0, with w(0) = w_s(0) = w_ss(L) = w_sss(L) = 0. The animation retains the first three bending modes and adds 1.8% modal damping. The first mode is 3 Hz, followed by approximately 18.8 Hz and 52.6 Hz. L = 1 m; EI/(ρA) is chosen to give the first frequency. No point mass is added at the tip; the horizontal top cap is a visual marker only.

The base servo is ideal: it supplies the force needed to maintain the prescribed base dynamics despite the flexible-frame reaction. This example illustrates residual vibration and tool-position error, not feedback instability or actuator saturation. The plots show tip deflection relative to the base, with no magnification; the drawn deformation is magnified threefold relative to translation. The schematic proportions are not a geometric scale drawing.

The PDE is evaluated through a finite modal approximation (coupled ODEs). The comparison is rigid versus flexible modelling; sufficiently detailed ODE models can also represent flexible dynamics.
